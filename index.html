Part 1

Tokenization & vocabulary

The tokenizer lowercases text, splits it into words and punctuation, and looks each one up in a fixed vocabulary. A word it has never seen is broken into the longest character pieces that do exist.

Training corpus

Vocabulary

How the vocabulary is built
  • [UNK] takes ID 0 and is the only special token.
  • Word-level tokens: the 20 most frequent words and punctuation marks in the corpus, most frequent first. Ties go to whichever appears first.
  • Character pieces: the 26 letters a to z, each written with a ## prefix.
  • Subword pieces: the most frequent two-letter pairs inside corpus words, enough to bring the pieces to 51 in total. The ## prefix keeps the piece ##i distinct from the word i.
  • Nothing is padded or duplicated to reach 72. If the corpus has fewer distinct words or letter pairs, the total comes out lower and the check above says so.

Part 2: Word Embeddings & Vector Space

A token ID is only a label. An embedding gives each token a list of numbers, and the training pushes tokens that appear in similar surroundings toward similar numbers.

Word embedding

The vector for one whole word, such as cat. It is learned from the words that appear around it.

Token embedding

The vector for any vocabulary entry: words, punctuation, and the character and subword pieces. Every one of the tokens has one.

Sentence embedding

One vector for a whole sentence, here the mean of its token vectors. It ignores word order.

Embedding dimension

How many numbers each vector holds. More dimensions can store finer differences but need more data to train well.

Train the embeddings

The method is skip-gram with negative sampling. For every token, the model learns to tell the tokens that really appear within two positions of it from five randomly drawn ones. Piece tokens learn from the words they are part of. Vectors start from a seeded random draw, so the same seed always gives the same result.

Changing the dimension retrains straight away. Epochs and seed apply when you press Retrain.

Token embeddings

Sentence embeddings

Each sentence vector is the mean of its token vectors. Two sentences made of the same tokens in a different order get the same vector.
